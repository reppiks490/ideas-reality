# RDX01 — Methods

1. Use complaint received/public time, not incident date.
2. Treat complaints as allegations, not confirmed defects.
3. Normalize by point-in-time model population/production.
4. Detect duplicate complaints without suppressing independent reports.
5. Prevent later recall language from leaking into earlier text features.
6. Store manufacturer communication date and NHTSA receipt/publication separately.
7. Classify routine TSB, diagnostic, warranty extension, service campaign, OTA, stop-sale and defect notice separately.
8. Archive investigation open/close states and all public document revisions.
9. Part 573 five-business-day clock starts after defect/noncompliance determination, not first failure.
10. Chronology facts are usable only when filing/amendment is public.
11. Version campaign scope, remedy and superseding recalls.
12. Separate produced, sold, dealer-held and in-service exposure.
13. Do not infer stop-sale outside exact legal/campaign context.
14. Archive interim/final remedy availability.
15. Model software/OTA and hardware campaigns separately.
16. Respect EWR monthly/quarterly availability.
17. Use only EWR fields actually public.
18. Require verified substantially-similar mapping for foreign-recall lead.
19. Version supplier/component/OEM-program entity mappings with confidence.
20. Before market alpha require OOS prediction of investigation, recall, scope expansion, remedy delay, stop-sale duration, completion, production interruption or cost.
