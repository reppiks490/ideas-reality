# RDX06 — Methods

1. Archive every monthly EIA-860M vintage; EIA explicitly says preliminary estimates can be corrected later without specific notice.
2. Use status and planned date available then, never latest project history.
3. Queue entry is not capacity; estimate phase-specific survival probability.
4. Separate nameplate MW from accredited/firm capacity.
5. Project delay/cancellation is a competing-risks problem.
6. Use project/developer/technology/region hierarchical priors.
7. Keep queue project identity stable through renames/ownership changes.
8. Preserve interconnection agreement, study phase and network upgrade milestones.
9. Network-upgrade costs/timing must be point-in-time.
10. Model restudies caused by project withdrawals/network changes.
11. Owner-planned retirement is not guaranteed retirement.
12. Archive retirement postponement/cancellation as positive supply revision.
13. Separate regulatory retention/emergency orders from voluntary owner revisions.
14. Large load announcements require commitment-strength classification.
15. In PJM, use firm/non-firm large-load methodology and public load adjustment revisions point-in-time.
16. Do not treat data-center requested MW as 100% utilization.
17. Pair load realization with substation/transmission/energization evidence.
18. Build zone-level rather than national-only balance.
19. Validate future-balance model against realized generation/load and capacity prices.
20. Before market alpha require OOS prediction of COD, retirement, realized load, reserve margin, fuel burn or capacity price.
